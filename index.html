Admin

University setup for your first week

This is not a complete arrival checklist. Address registration, health-insurance status and, where required, residence permission are separate tasks.

1

For RWTH students: request your BlueCard

Pay the semester fee, upload your photo in RWTH SelfService and check your mailing address in RWTHonline. The BlueCard is your student ID and Mensa payment card.

Not arrived? Check fee, photo and postal address, then contact the RWTH BlueCard Service Point; the card is not your semester ticket.

2

For RWTH students: set up your university email

Redeem your ConnectMe coupon and open your RWTH mailbox. Important university and semester-ticket messages arrive there.

No email access? Make sure you redeemed ConnectMe - activation can take up to 48 hours; missing coupon: contact the IT ServiceDesk.

3

For RWTH and FH Aachen students: use the Mensa

Use your BlueCard or FH Karte to pay at the Studierendenwerk dining halls. Autoload is optional; you can also top up at the machines.

No payment chip? Ask about the Studierendenwerk student guest card (deposit + credit) and show your student ID for the student price.

4

For RWTH students: activate your semester ticket

After enrolment and ConnectMe activation, ASEAG sends a linking code to your RWTH email. Register in naveo once with that code; check that a valid ticket appears before travelling.

No naveo code? Check enrollment, semester fee, ConnectMe and your RWTH mailbox, then email semesterticket@aseag.de. Code used but no ticket: hallo@naveo.info - do not delete the account.

naveo unusable? RWTH AStA explains the physical chip-card ticket application; it takes up to two weeks and is not a temporary ticket.

  • The Deutschlandsemesterticket covers local and regional transport in second class, not ICE/IC/EC. It is funded through the semester fee, not a free ticket.
  • Do not board without a valid ticket. If your semester ticket is not ready, buy another valid fare first.
  • Bus stops can have several platforms (H1, H2 and so on). Check the platform for your direction.

For FH Aachen students: use FH-specific university instructions for your FH Karte, email and semester ticket; the RWTH steps above do not apply.

Admin in Germany

The national admin tasks after you arrive: register your address, sort out health insurance and residence status, and understand official letters. University setup steps are in your city's admin guide.

Money in Germany: bank account, health insurance, liability insurance, SIM card and SCHUFA guides.

Register your address

After moving in, register your address within two weeks at the local registration office (Bürgeramt or Einwohnermeldeamt). Bring your ID or passport and a signed Wohnungsgeberbestätigung from the housing provider; the rental contract does not replace it. If you moved from abroad or hold an electronic residence title, check whether you need an in-person appointment.

No confirmation on time? Contact the registration office directly. Never register an address you do not live at.

Check health insurance and residence status

Ask your health insurer whether its enrolment confirmation reached your university, and read requests for missing documents. Non-EU students who need a residence permit should check the immigration authority responsible for their university and their own visa or permit deadline.

Insurance proof missing? Ask your insurer to send the electronic student notification to the university. Residence deadline close? Contact the immigration authority directly.

Official letters: what to do

  • Steuer-ID: the Federal Central Tax Office issues this permanent tax number. Keep the letter. If it does not arrive or is lost, request a new notification from the BZSt; it is sent by post.
  • Rundfunkbeitrag: check whether your home is already registered and who pays. Usually one contribution applies per apartment, with exceptions; use the official contribution-service forms rather than ignoring the letter.
  • Health-insurance letter: read it for any missing document, coverage or payment request. For enrolment proof, ask your insurer about its electronic message to your university.
  • Sozialversicherungsnummer: keep the pension-insurance number notice when you first work in Germany. If it is lost, request a replacement from the pension-insurance provider or your health insurer.
  • Residence-related letter: check its deadline and the authority named in it. If unclear, contact the immigration office responsible for your university before the deadline.

Housing in Germany

Find a room. Know what you are signing.

Before you sign

  • Kaution is a refundable security deposit. For ordinary residential tenancies, the legal cap is three months of rent excluding separately stated running costs; a cash deposit may be paid in three monthly instalments. Check your contract before paying.
  • Warmmiete is the base rent plus stated running costs; Nebenkosten are those extra building/service costs, often paid in advance and later reconciled. Ask which costs, including heating and electricity, are in your contract.
  • Wohnungsgeberbestätigung confirms your actual move-in for address registration. The owner or main tenant signs it. It is separate from the rental contract.
  • Scam warning: verify the person and the room before sending money or sensitive documents. Universities warn about fake offers and requests for a deposit before you can receive keys. Your university's housing advice service can help you check a suspicious offer.

Bank accounts in Germany

A Girokonto (current account) is the first thing to set up: rent, health insurance and wages all run through it.

Student Girokonto at a Sparkasse

Sparkasse offers a Girokonto for students and trainees with an app, contactless payments and around 20,000 ATMs across Germany. It is free at many branches: each Sparkasse sets its own account conditions and fees, so ask your local branch whether it offers the free student account and whether an age limit applies. (Source: sparkasse.de, checked on October 19, 2026.) Turned down at one branch? Try another branch or book online; an online bank works as a stopgap and you can move to a Sparkasse later.

Set up rent as a standing order

Set your rent as a Dauerauftrag (standing order) from your account so it goes out automatically every month. A clean rent payment record matters when you apply for flats.

Credit card (optional)

The PAYBACK Visa costs EUR 29 per year. Payments are fee-free worldwide and it collects Payback points on every payment; points do not expire while the card is active. Cash withdrawals are fee-free abroad but cost 2% (minimum EUR 5) within Germany. (Source: payback.de, checked on October 19, 2026.)

Our editorial pick

A Sparkasse student Girokonto. Criteria: free for students at many branches per Sparkasse's own page (each Sparkasse sets its own conditions), a branch and ATM network that covers every city in this guide, and a name every landlord and employer recognizes. This is an editorial preference, not a price ranking.

Health insurance in Germany

Health insurance is mandatory: you must prove coverage when you enrol at university. This page describes the option types; it is not medical advice.

Statutory (public) insurance

Most students use statutory insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung). The legally required basic coverage - doctor visits, medicines, emergencies, hospital stays - is identical no matter which Krankenkasse you choose. As a dated example, TK lists its 2026 student contribution at EUR 141.16 per month up to age 23 and EUR 146.29 from age 23 without children, including care insurance, and notes the contribution barely differs between Krankenkassen. The student rate normally runs until the end of the semester in which you turn 30. (Sources: daad.de and tk.de, checked on October 19, 2026.)

Private insurance

Private plans can offer more benefits but are significantly more expensive and usually require paying bills yourself first and claiming reimbursement. Switching back to statutory insurance later is hard. (Source: daad.de, checked on October 19, 2026.)

Enrolment confirmation

Your Krankenkasse reports your insurance status to the university electronically; enrolment is not complete without it. With enrolment you are also automatically covered by the university's accident insurance for on-campus and course-related activities. (Source: daad.de, checked on October 19, 2026.)

Dental + vision cover (optional)

Optional, but if you wear glasses, look at the cover. BarmeniaGothaer offers supplementary dental insurance and eye cover (Mehr Sehen). Check the current premium, benefit limits, conditions and reimbursement interval before buying.

Our editorial pick

Statutory insurance as a student, from any Krankenkasse you like. Criteria: the basic coverage is legally identical everywhere, the discounted student rate is the standard compliant option (the TK figures above are a dated example, not a recommendation), and every Krankenkasse can report your status to the university. This is an editorial preference, not medical or financial advice.

Liability insurance (Haftpflicht)

Private Haftpflichtversicherung covers damage you cause to other people or their things. Most people in Germany have one, and it costs a few euros per month.

What it covers

The classic cases: you break a friend's laptop, lose a landlord's key, or scratch a parked car with your bike. Without insurance you pay these yourself; liability insurance pays them for you.

What to check before buying

Coverage sum (aim high - damage to people can be very expensive), whether lost keys are included, and the deductible. Read the tariff page, not just the price.

Our editorial pick

Die Haftpflichtkasse, tariff Einfach Besser. Criteria: a specialist liability insurer, and the tariff covers personal, property and financial damage up to EUR 50 million per its own tariff page. The page lists no fixed premium - the price depends on your details, so get a quote there. (Source: haftpflichtkasse.de, checked on October 19, 2026.) This is an editorial preference, not a price ranking.

Monthly options

If you prefer monthly billing, every network (Telekom, Vodafone, O2) and the supermarket brands sell monthly prepaid packs. Check the current price and data volume before buying - offers change often.

SCHUFA explained

Understand SCHUFA

SCHUFA is Germany's credit record, and landlords often ask for a SCHUFA report when you apply for a flat. You do not need to open extra products to build a score: SCHUFA's own advice is to pay your bills on time and keep your finances simple. A clean payment history on the accounts you already have is what helps over time. You can check your data in the SCHUFA app (Android / iPhone) or request a free data copy from SCHUFA.

SCHUFA offers a free data copy (Datenkopie) at schufa.de. (Source: schufa.de, checked on October 19, 2026.)

Jobs

Germany has a legal minimum wage, so even a simple job pays properly. Right now it is €13.90 an hour, going up to €14.60 in January 2027.

Office & corporate

Working student (Werkstudent) jobs in companies. Great for your CV and your career after the degree.

Hospitality & retail

Restaurants, cafés, shops. Fastest way to earn, and it helps you learn German a lot.

Know the different work-hour rules

  • Immigration rule for non-EU students: you can work 140 full days or 280 half days per year without extra permission. The exact rule is noted in your residence title.
  • Working-student (Werkstudent) rule: if you work up to 20 hours a week during the semester, your studies count as your main occupation and you keep the working-student privileges in social insurance. In semester breaks, and for evening or weekend work, more hours are allowed under the 26-week rule.
  • Student health insurance: student rates apply while your studies are clearly your main occupation. If you regularly work more than 20 hours a week, check with your health insurer whether you move into employee insurance.

Office & corporate jobs

  1. Your resume must be in German format. Copy the layout from the sample resume.
  2. Volume beats everything for working student roles. Do not wait for the perfect job.
  3. Find jobs on LinkedIn, StepStone, wherever. But always apply directly on the company's own careers site.
  4. Always attach an application package in the "Additional Documents" section.

The reference letter must follow the German format

A recommendation letter in the style used back home will not land well. Ask your old employer for an Arbeitszeugnis in the German format. It can be written in English: on company letterhead, with a signature and the company stamp.

German references use fixed grading phrases. Recruiters read them like marks, so the letter should use this language:

Very good (sehr gut)

"always to our fullest satisfaction" (stets zu unserer vollsten Zufriedenheit)

Good (gut)

"always to our full satisfaction" (stets zu unserer vollen Zufriedenheit)

Aim for "very good" wording in the performance and conduct sections. See the sample Arbeitszeugnis for a full example in English.

Hospitality & retail jobs

You can find these jobs on platforms like Indeed, Zenjob and others. But the best way is to just walk into restaurants and hotels and ask if they are hiring anyone part time.

  1. Every day, pick one area of the city.
  2. Start with the food court at a mall. After that, areas further from the center are better: fewer students asking.
  3. Walk into each restaurant and hotel and ask for a job, in German.
  4. It doesn't matter if you speak fluently, what matters is to show you can try.

University setup for your first week

This is not a complete arrival checklist. Address registration, health-insurance status and, where required, residence permission are separate tasks.

1

Register your Tübingen address

You must register within two weeks of moving in. It is free. Bring your ID or passport and your landlord's confirmation (Wohnungsgeberbescheinigung). Late registration is an offence and can be fined.

You can register online with an ID card with online function. In person, the Bürgerbüro (Schmiedtorstraße 4) works by appointment only; book online.

2

For University of Tübingen students: get your student card

The Studierendensekretariat posts your chip card after enrolment. It is your student ID, opens some buildings and pays at the Mensa and at copiers.

After each re-registration, update the semester print at a self-service terminal. A lost card costs €15 to replace and unused credit is not refunded.

3

For students: use the Mensa

Pay with your student card or a Mensakarte at Studierendenwerk dining halls and cafeterias. Autoload is optional; you can also top up at the machines on campus.

4

For University of Tübingen students: buy the naldo semester ticket

The semester ticket covers the whole naldo network and is sold only for the full semester. Buy it in the naldo Studi-Web-Shop after you have enrolled or re-registered. It comes as a mobile ticket with a Print@Home copy, works only with your current student ID and cannot be transferred.

  • naldo's tariff table, valid from 1 October 2025, lists €161.10 per semester. The university page still shows an older price, so confirm the current price in the shop.
  • Without a ticket, your student ID is valid on naldo buses and trains Monday to Friday from 19:00 and all day at weekends and on Baden-Württemberg public holidays. The university page notes a transition rule; check it.
